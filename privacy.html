Privacy policy

Your players stay on your phone

Effective 24 October 2026

Joy is a party game app for iPhone and Android made by Factory Labs (“we”, “us”). This policy explains what Joy keeps on your phone, the little the iPhone app sends us, and what it never does.

In short

  • Joy has no accounts and no sign-in. Player names and everything you type in a game stay on your phone.
  • The games play without the internet. When the phone is online, the iPhone app sends us usage counts and crash reports through Google's Firebase, so we know which games get played and what to fix.
  • No ads, no advertising identifier, no tracking across apps. We don't sell or share anything for advertising.
  • If you send us feedback, we get what you write and attach. Deleting the app deletes everything it kept on your phone.

What Joy keeps on your phone

To work, Joy saves a few things in its own private storage on your device:

  • Your players: the names you type, the colour each one wears, their order, and who's playing tonight.
  • Your settings: language, theme, whether you've seen the welcome page, and the last mode, length and intensity you picked for each game.
  • Card history: which cards each deck has dealt, so the next night starts with ones you haven't seen.
  • A feedback draft: a message you started in Settings and haven't sent yet.

None of this is sent to us.

What the iPhone app sends

The iPhone app uses Firebase, a Google service, for three things. None of them includes player names or anything you type in a game.

  • Usage counts (Firebase Analytics): that the welcome page was finished; that a game started, with which game, length, intensity and mode, and how many are playing; that a game finished or was played again; that the random game button picked one; that a game failed to open; that the app asked iOS for the rating prompt; and that feedback was sent, with how many files it had. Each one carries the app's language and theme, and Firebase adds basic facts such as the device model, iOS version, app version, an approximate location worked out from the IP address, and a random ID for this installation of the app.
  • Crash reports (Firebase Crashlytics): when the app crashes, or can't load or save your players or card history, it sends a report of what the code was doing, with the device model, iOS version, app version, the app's language and theme, and the installation ID.
  • Remote settings (Firebase Remote Config): the app checks with Firebase for updated settings, such as whether feedback is switched on. The request carries the installation ID.

The installation ID isn't tied to your name, Apple ID or phone number, and a new one is made if you delete the app and install it again. Google processes this data for us under the Firebase terms; see Privacy and Security in Firebase.

Feedback

Send feedback in Settings delivers your message to our team's private chat on Telegram. It carries what you write, your email if you choose to leave one, any photos or videos you attach (made smaller, with location and other metadata removed), and your device model, iOS version, app version and app language. We use it only to read your message and to reply. Telegram stores it under its own privacy policy. Joy only ever sees the photos and videos you pick to attach.

What Joy never does

  • It never asks you to create an account or sign in.
  • It shows no ads, reads no advertising identifier, and doesn't track you across other apps or websites.
  • It never sends player names or anything you type in a game.
  • It doesn't access your contacts, camera, microphone or location, or any photo you don't pick yourself.

The Android app

The Android app, coming to Google Play, collects nothing. It asks for no permission except vibration, not even internet access. If that changes, we'll update this policy before that version is released.

Motion and vibration

The cards lean and catch the light as you tilt your phone, and in Who Am I? the phone feels when you tip it down or up on your forehead. To do that, Joy reads the phone's motion sensor while it's needed. The readings are used on the spot and are never stored or sent. Vibration only plays feedback on the phone.

Language on first launch

The first time Joy opens, it picks English or Georgian by looking at the phone's language and region (and, on Android, the SIM's country) on the device itself. You can change the language at any time in Settings.

Backups

If you back up your phone to iCloud, Google or a computer, the system's backup may include Joy's data along with your other apps, under Apple's or Google's own terms. Restoring a backup brings your players back.

Deleting your data

Swipe left on a player to delete them, or clear the whole list in Players. To remove everything Joy has kept on your phone, delete the app. To have us delete feedback you've sent, write to us at the address below.

Children

Joy is made for groups of friends and isn't directed at children. We don't knowingly collect personal information from children.

The App Store and Google Play

You download Joy from Apple's App Store or from Google Play. They may collect information about downloads and purchases under their own privacy policies, which we don't control.

This website

factorylabs.app/joy is a static site hosted on GitHub Pages, with no cookies, analytics or trackers. It remembers your language in your browser's local storage, which never leaves your browser. GitHub may log visitors' IP addresses for security, and the page's fonts come from Google Fonts, which receives your IP address when it serves them.

Changes

If what Joy collects changes, we'll update this policy before that version is released and change the date at the top.

Contact

Questions about privacy: contact@factorylabs.app
